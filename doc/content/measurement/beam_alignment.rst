.. _beam_alignment:

=======================
Electron beam alignment
=======================

The incidence angle of the electron beam on the sample has a significant impact
on the outcome of a |LEED-IV| measurement. Even a small angular error can lead
to substantially worse R-factors. To ensure that symmetry-equivalent
diffraction spots are indeed equivalent, the beam must be perpendicular to the
sample surface. It is therefore worth spending considerable time to ensure that
the sample is properly aligned. The following procedure must be performed
whenever anything about the shielding, the electron gun, or the compensation
coil setup changes.

Note that you can use the camera live view in the **Devices** menu to assist
with the alignment process. If you select your camera there and activate
auto-contrast, you will struggle less with brightness changes when varying the
beam energy.

.. _field_compensation:

Magnetic field compensation
===========================

If the vacuum chamber is not shielded against magnetic fields, e.g., the
Earth’s magnetic field, you need to compensate for these fields. This is
typically done by two coils outside the chamber, which create magnetic fields
roughly perpendicular to the electron beam and to each other. The easiest way
to determine the compensation fields required (the coil currents) is measuring
the beam position as a function of electron energy and ensuring that the beam
does not move when the energy is changed. This procedure requires that the
electron source itself is properly aligned, i.e., the direction of the electron
beam it emits does not depend on energy.

For this method you need a plate on an x-y manipulator, with a small hole
(≈1 mm) at the center, at the position where the sample would normally be
mounted. There must be an electron collector behind the hole plate (e.g., a
sample plate with a hole and the filament of an electron beam heater underneath
acting as the electron collector). If you are not using the "I_sample @+33 V"
input of the ViPErLEED hardware controller for the current measurement, apply a
positive bias to the electron collector to prevent secondary electron emission
from interfering with the alignment process.

Begin by placing the electron collector under the electron gun so that it is
visually aligned. Tilt the hole plate such that the electron beam is
perpendicular to it. Set the currents of the compensation coils to the
previously determined values or 0, if no previous values are known. Turn on
your LEED optics and increase the beam energy to at least 1000 eV. While
measuring the electron collector current, move the sample plate in the plane
perpendicular to the beam direction and look for the current maximum. Once you
have narrowed down the position of the maximum, adjust the tilt and search for
the angle that produces the largest electron collector current. After
determining the angle, repeat the process and determine position and angle
again. If these values no longer change, decrease the beam energy to a low
value such as 30 eV.

Adjust the coil currents until the electron collector current reaches a maximum
again. At this point, the magnetic field inside the chamber generated by the
coils should roughly compensate the influence of external magnetic fields.
After locating the maximum, increase the beam energy back to 1000 eV.

Repeat the position and angle calibration at high beam energy. The position and
angle should not change much, as magnetic fields are less impactful at higher
electron energies. Afterwards, repeat the compensation coil calibration at low
beam energies. Continue alternating between these two energies until the
changes become negligible. Note down the final position, angle, and coil
currents. Use these values for all future |LEED-IV| measurements unless the
setup changes. If the compensation coils are moved, the entire calibration must
be repeated.

Sample alignment
================

|LEED-IV| data are usually recorded at perpendicular incidence. Even a slight
tilt of a few tenths of a degree has a substantial influence on the |LEED-IV|
data. Therefore, it is best to have a manipulator that allows the sample to be
tilted in both directions. The following alignment procedure should be
performed whenever a new sample is inserted into the manipulator.

Ensure that the magnetic field compensation coils are active, if required. Turn
on the LEED optics and slowly increase the beam energy. For a rough alignment,
the diffraction spots should converge toward the point directly beneath the
electron gun. If they do not, then the sample is not perpendicular to the
electron beam. Adjust the angle until all spots converge toward the electron
gun (the middle of the LEED screen).

Once the optimal angle has been found, reduce the beam energy again. Slowly
increase it until you see a set of diffraction spots that are clearly
symmetry-equivalent. Find an energy where these spots vanish into the
background and sweep the energy back and forth around this value. Symmetry-
equivalent spots must disappear at exactly the same energy. If they do not,
slightly adjust the sample angle until they disappear at the same energy. At
this stage, only very small angle adjustments (fractions of a degree) should be
necessary. After the two spots vanish simultaneously, select a different pair
of symmetry-equivalent spots and repeat the process to ensure that the
alignment was successful. If it turns out that the tilt required for
perpendicular incidence depends on the electron energy, this is most likely
caused by residual magnetic or electric fields. Ensure that there are no
magnetic fields (see :ref:`magnetic field compensation <field_compensation>`)
and there are no insulating surfaces (insulators, surfaces of oxidized Al or
stainless steel with chromium oxide at the surface) where charge can accumulate
and deflect electrons.
